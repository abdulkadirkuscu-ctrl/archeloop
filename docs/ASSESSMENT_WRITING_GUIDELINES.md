Purpose

This document defines the standards for writing assessment questions within the ArcheLoop Assessment.

Its purpose is to ensure every assessment question measures the intended developmental construct while remaining fully consistent with the ArcheLoop Framework.

This document should be used whenever assessment questions are created, reviewed, or updated.

It serves as the writing standard for:

Assessment development
Future revisions
Scoring validation
Report development
AI-assisted question generation
Psychometric review

Every assessment question should follow these standards.

1. Assessment Philosophy

The ArcheLoop Assessment does not measure personality.

It measures the current state of psychological development across twelve Developmental Capacities.

Every question exists to identify the person's current developmental pattern rather than define who they are.

The purpose of the Assessment is to help people:

Understand → Interrupt → Integrate

2. Developmental Structure

The Assessment measures twelve Developmental Capacities.

Archetype	Developmental Capacities
Sovereign (Fire)	Visibility • Worth • Action
Magician (Air)	Expression • Truth • Clarity
Lover (Water)	Vulnerability • Connection • Emotional Regulation
Warrior (Earth)	Boundaries • Trust • Vitality

Every assessment question belongs to one Developmental Capacity only.

Questions should never measure multiple capacities simultaneously.

3. Healthy & Protective Functioning

Each Developmental Capacity is measured through two complementary states:

Healthy Functioning

Measures the availability of the developmental capacity.

Healthy Functioning consists of:

Healthy Capacity

Measures the person's internal psychological ability.

Examples

I trust...
I know...
I recognise...
I feel safe...
I allow myself...
Healthy Expression

Measures how the developmental capacity appears in everyday behaviour.

Examples

I speak...
I express...
I connect...
I respond...
I take action...
I set boundaries...

Healthy Expression should always describe an observable behaviour.

Protective Functioning

Protective Functioning develops when Healthy Functioning becomes psychologically unavailable.

Protective Functioning always appears through one of three Protective Formations.

4. The Three Protective Formations

These are the foundation of the ArcheLoop Assessment.

Every Shadow Loop belongs to exactly one Protective Formation.

Collapse

Healthy functioning becomes psychologically unavailable.

Typical movement:

withdraw
hide
freeze
suppress
disconnect
become small
avoid

Collapse answers:

What becomes unavailable?

Compensate

Healthy functioning no longer feels sufficient.

The psyche creates a protective substitute.

Typical movement:

prove
perform
control
idealise
rationalise
impress
perfect

Compensate answers:

What becomes performative?

Collide

Two psychological systems activate simultaneously.

Internal conflict interrupts healthy functioning.

Typical movement:

stall
overthink
flood
react
become stuck
oscillate

Collide answers:

What becomes psychologically conflicted?

5. Canonical Shadow Loop Structure

This structure is fixed.

Archetype	Collapse	Compensate	Collide
Sovereign	Dimmed Light	Paper Crown	Stalled Flame
Magician	Blank Page	Smoky Mirrors	Mind Maze
Lover	Emotional Lockdown	Fantasy Fog	Flooded Waters
Warrior	Compliance	Fortress	Barren Ground

Questions should reinforce the defining movement of the assigned Protective Formation.

6. Measurement Model

Every Developmental Capacity contains exactly five questions.

Healthy
Healthy Capacity

Measures internal psychological ability.

Healthy Expression

Measures observable everyday behaviour.

Shadow Loop

Every Shadow Loop contains three independent forms of evidence.

Protective Belief

Measures:

assumptions
expectations
predictions
interpretations

Typical language:

I believe...
It feels safer...
I expect...
Protective Emotion

Measures the emotional experience associated with the Protective Formation.

Examples:

shame
disappointment
frustration
numbness
overwhelm
helplessness

The emotion should not simply restate the belief.

Protective Behaviour

Measures the observable behaviour maintaining psychological safety.

Typical behaviours include:

hiding
withdrawing
proving
performing
controlling
rationalising
overthinking
reacting
avoiding

Behaviour should describe something another person could reasonably observe.

7. Domain Anchoring Principle

Healthy and Shadow questions should remain anchored to the same developmental domain.

Example

Visibility

Healthy

"I allow myself to take up space."

↓

Shadow

"I feel ashamed when I take up space."

Both questions measure the same developmental domain from opposite directions.

This improves construct clarity and report interpretation.

8. One Construct Principle

Each question measures one construct only.

Avoid combining:

multiple behaviours
multiple emotions
multiple developmental capacities
multiple archetypes

One question.

One construct.

9. Capacity vs Expression Principle

Healthy Capacity measures internal ability.

Healthy Expression measures observable behaviour.

If a Healthy Expression cannot be observed externally, it is probably measuring Healthy Capacity instead.

10. Lens Independence Principle

Every Shadow Loop should contain three independent psychological lenses.

Protective Belief

↓

Protective Emotion

↓

Protective Behaviour

Each lens should contribute unique information.

The Emotion should not simply repeat the Belief.

The Behaviour should not simply restate the Emotion.

Lens Independence Test

Ask:

Does the Belief describe what the person believes?
Does the Emotion describe what the person experiences because of that belief?
Does the Behaviour describe what the person does because of the belief and emotion?

If replacing "I feel..." with "I believe..." produces essentially the same sentence, the Belief and Emotion are measuring the same construct and one should be rewritten.

11. Language Principles

Questions should:

use everyday language
avoid clinical terminology
avoid diagnostic language
avoid judgement
avoid absolutes such as "always" and "never"
avoid double-barrelled wording
avoid leading questions
avoid social desirability cues

Questions should describe psychological processes rather than identity.

12. Writing Guidelines

Aim for:

8–16 words where practical.
Maximum approximately 20 words unless additional context is essential.

Questions should:

read naturally aloud
focus on one idea
avoid unnecessary qualifiers
remain emotionally neutral
be easy to understand without psychological knowledge
13. Validation Checklist

Before approving any question, confirm:

☐ Measures one Developmental Capacity.
☐ Measures one construct.
☐ Fits the assigned Protective Formation.
☐ Fits the assigned measurement lens.
☐ Uses clear everyday language.
☐ Contains no double-barrelled wording.
☐ Can be understood without psychological jargon.
☐ Adds unique information to the assessment.
☐ Supports the corresponding Shadow Loop.
☐ Aligns with the ArcheLoop Framework Architecture.
